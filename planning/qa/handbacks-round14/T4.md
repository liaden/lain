# Hand-back — T4: a zero last-turn usage is absence, not a measurement, in Accounting

**Status: fix round applied.** Round 1 was returned REQUEST-CHANGES with two BLOCKERs; the panel
was right, and both are fixed below, plus the follow-on amendment moving the spelling to
`.positive?`. Scope held throughout: only `lib/lain/agent/accounting.rb` and
`spec/lain/agent/accounting_spec.rb` were touched. No git command was run.

## The defect that came back, and why round 1 missed it

Round 1 gated on `usage.zero?` — a predicate over **four** fields — while writing
`usage.total_input_tokens`, a sum over **three**. `Usage.new(input_tokens: 0, output_tokens: 250)`
therefore passes the gate and writes `0` over a standing reading. Round 1 not only shipped that,
it *pinned* it with an example asserting the clobber was correct, on a rationale
("the response billed output, so its zero input is a measurement") that does not survive the
arithmetic. Every example round 1 added was hand-built from `input:`/`output:` keywords, which is
exactly why it never tried the shape a real decoder emits.

## Final state of the code

### `lib/lain/agent/accounting.rb`

```ruby
def take_reading(usage)
  reading = usage.total_input_tokens
  @last_turn_usage = reading if reading.positive?
end
```

- **Gate quantity** is now `total_input_tokens` — the same three-field sum being written, held in
  **one local** so the gate and the assignment cannot drift apart. That is the structural version
  of the ruling "the gate must match the quantity being written".
- **Gate spelling** is `.positive?`, per the amendment, not "unless `.zero?`".
- `#observe` still does `@usage +=` and still writes the `Telemetry::TurnUsage` record
  unconditionally; suppression touches the *reading* only.
- **Comment rewritten** (the old one recorded the wrong rationale as doctrine). It now says: zero
  is *how missing billing information arrives* (every nullable wire field normalizes to it), so a
  response reporting no input tokens has said nothing about window fullness; writing its zero
  drops `Agent#occupancy` to 0.0 and stops `Need` firing. It states why gating on the whole
  four-field value would let precisely the Ollama shape through, and why ignoring output is
  *correct* here — occupancy asks what filled the window on the way IN, and this turn's output is
  accounted as the next turn's input. A final paragraph gives the `.positive?` reason: neither
  `Usage` nor `StatusFeed::JournaledUsage` forbids a negative sum (`Integer()` and `#to_i` both
  accept one), that is the single input on which the two spellings disagree, so both producers of
  this reading say `.positive?` and the policy has one spelling rather than two that merely happen
  to agree. No ticket references anywhere.
- **Docstring fixed.** "the most recent response's billed-on-the-way-in tokens" became "the
  billed-on-the-way-in tokens of the most recent response **that reported any**", with a pointer
  to `#take_reading` for why a response carrying none is skipped rather than believed. The
  `@return` clause on nil-vs-zero was already accurate and is unchanged.

### `spec/lain/agent/accounting_spec.rb` — 16 examples (was 11 before this card)

- **Deleted** the round-1 example that pinned the bug ("takes a zero reading when output was
  billed, since that is a measurement"). Removed, not adapted. Its sibling at `input: 7, output: 0

- **Fixture realism**, per the ruling: a `ollama_body_missing_prompt_eval_count` helper holding a

- New examples: the fixture-shape guard (`total_input_tokens == 0` beside `output_tokens == 250`,
  so the fixture cannot silently stop exercising the case); the end-to-end clobber guard (a
  900,000-token reading survives that response); and the negative-sum guard below.
- The four Gherkin ACs remain covered: standing reading survives a zero turn; the cumulative total
  folds both; the `turn_usage` record is still journaled with its own all-zero usage; absence

## Surprises

- The round-1 rationale was self-consistent and still wrong — "billed for output, therefore its
  zero input is a measurement" reads fine until you notice `Usage` has no way to express absence
  at all, which makes the claim unfalsifiable. The lesson is the panel's: the fixture realism,
  not the reasoning, is what would have caught it, and it was one helper away.
- `Ollama::Decoding#build_usage` is private on a mixin whose only stateful collaborator (`@journal`,
  used by `#note_prose_tool_call`) is untouched by `build_usage`, so `Object.new.extend(...)` is a
  safe way to drive the real decoder from a unit spec without standing up a provider.
